      Projects

      A project is the whole bed: the drawings, where they sit, pens and speeds.

        Layers and pens

        Each ink layer is plotted in turn; the plot pauses for a pen change between layers. Settings left empty use the job defaults. Pressure is a fraction from pen-down (0, heaviest) toward pen-up, at most 0.9.

        Open a drawing to see its layers.

        Filled shapes

        Shapes with a fill colour, by colour. A pen cannot fill, so a fill becomes lines: hatching (parallel lines, optionally crossed) or contour rings that follow the outline inward. The fill lines get a layer of their own colour above, with its own pen.

        Pen tests

        Test sheets and their results belong to one pen type. A result for a ballpoint says nothing about a marker.

        Live writing

        Type a line, press Write, and the plotter writes it as you go. Needs the carriage parked at S, like a plot. Use a font, or your own handwriting once you have captured samples.

        Sport mode is above the tested speed (40 mm/s). The motors can lose steps and the position is not trusted afterwards. Use it rarely.

        SPORT MODE: the pad drives the plotter at up to 100 mm/s. Re-park the carriage afterwards.

        Control

        Position unknown

        Move the carriage from here: jog, go to a point, pen up and down, or set S exactly on the paper corner. Starts like a plot, with the carriage parked by hand at S and the pen up. The position is counted from S; nothing can be read back from this firmware.

        Arrow keys on the bed jog one step too.

        Finishing lifts the pen and switches the motors off. Finish at S travels back first.

        Calibrate steps per mm

        With control taken and paper under the pen: draw two ticks a known distance apart, measure between them with a ruler, and save. Do X and Y separately.

        Paper

        S is the paper's top-left corner. Changing it means parking the carriage at the new corner.

        Pens

        Each pen profile: its ink colour and line width (drawn that way in Ink view), and the speed and pressure a layer takes when you choose it. Test results recorded from the sheets are kept.

              Writing pad

              How plotting works here

              1. Prepare (optional): drop an image, 3D model or SVG, or start from text. Build a recipe (adjust, method, clean up), check the result in ink, choose pens, and send it to Plot. A finished SVG can skip this.
              2. In Plot, add drawings from the Library. Several can share the bed.
              3. Arrange them: drag to move, drag a handle to resize (sides stretch, corners keep proportions), drag the round handle to turn. Or type the numbers under Arrange. Everything must stay inside the dashed canvas.
              4. Set pens and speeds per layer in Layers.
              5. Preview checks the plan with the plotter's own rules. Simulate runs it on a virtual machine.
              6. Switch the plotter on with the motors off, slide the carriage by hand to S (the paper's top-left), pen up.
              7. Start plot, confirm, and watch. Between layers it pauses for a pen change.

              Stopping

              This firmware (EBB 2.0.1) has no emergency stop and no position readback. Stop finishes the move already sent (normally within 3 s), lifts the pen and releases the motors; the carriage stays where it stopped. Return to start finishes the current stroke and travels back to S. For an instant stop, switch the plotter off.

              The carriage position shown is the commanded one, counted from S. If the carriage was bumped or the motors were off, it is wrong until you park it again.
